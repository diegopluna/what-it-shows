The three problems

parser: the shared parser reads every date month first. A tenant that writes the day first books 7 April and gets 4 July. It reaches every channel, at every tenant on file.

hint: booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type month first, as the hint tells them, and the form reads what they typed the tenant’s way, day first. Only reports through the form, only at tenants on booking-v3. Fixing the parser does not touch it: the hint is wrong, not the reading. Retiring booking-v3 ends it.

shift: the date slips by one day. Day and month stay put: Tuesday 12 May shows as Monday 11 May. Every booking in June one day off; the same meeting on Tuesday in Lisbon and Monday in Toronto; an invite that arrives before the booking. That is a time-zone or daylight-saving conversion, not a date order. The desk listed these reports in its notes, then filed them under parser by tenant, so they count there too. Reading dates day first does not move a date by one day: this one is still open.

How they ended up in one ticket

  • Subject lines. Decision 0001 merges on subject alone. “Wrong day on the invite” fits all three problems, so all three were merged as one.
  • Intake’s tag. intakeTag is a guess from the reporter’s browser. Everyone on an en-US browser got hint, whatever their tenant runs. The cause depends on the tenant’s locale and form (locales.ts).
  • The old table. The #4471 cause table marks the biggest group as the ticket and the rest “no ticket of its own”, and footnotes the one-day moves inside parser.
  • Overlapping export pages. The same report comes twice; count each id once.

When you triage the next one

  1. Count unique ids, not rows.
  2. Place each report by tenant and channel (causes.ts), not by subject or intake tag.
  3. A report that is neither a flipped date nor a booking-v3 form entry is its own problem until someone shows otherwise.
